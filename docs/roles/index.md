# Explore AI roles

An AI job title is a starting point for research. The more useful question is **what the person is expected to own**: a customer deployment, an application, a model, a product decision, an evaluation, or a risk process.

This atlas compares eight role families. Some titles are emerging; others are established roles whose responsibilities increasingly include generative AI. Employers use the same title differently, and small teams often combine several of these jobs. These profiles describe typical patterns, not a universal classification or a list of current vacancies.

| [AI solutions architect](/roles/solutions-architect) | Design systems that meet business and technical constraints | A justified architecture and implementation plan | A design document covering security, tradeoffs, cost, and operations |
| [AI product manager](/roles/product-manager) | Decide what to build, for whom, and how to judge it | A validated product plan and measurable launch | User research, a prioritized spec, and an evaluation plan |

| [AI research engineer](/roles/research-engineer) | Make research ideas testable and reproducible | An experiment, research system, or model implementation | A paper reproduction with baselines and documented limitations |
| [AI governance and safety specialist](/roles/governance-specialist) | Identify risks and organize responsible controls | A risk assessment, control process, or safety review | A system inventory, risk register, and evidence-backed review |

## Titles you may also encounter

**Applied AI engineer, LLM engineer, agent engineer, and generative AI engineer** can overlap with AI engineering. Read whether the work centers on application integration, model training, or research.

**AI deployment strategist, AI implementation engineer, and AI solutions engineer** can overlap with forward-deployed work. Some positions are primarily technical; others focus on consulting, sales support, or adoption.

**AI assurance, AI red teaming, responsible AI, and model risk** describe related but distinct specialties. A security tester, policy specialist, and model evaluator need different depth, even if all contribute to safer systems.

**Prompt engineer** can describe focused experimentation and workflow design, but prompting is also a skill within many other roles. Check the full responsibilities before treating it as a separate career ladder.

## Choose a direction

Start with a role whose daily work you would enjoy, then read two adjacent profiles. If you like building applications, compare AI engineer with ML engineer. If you like understanding users and explaining systems, compare forward-deployed engineering with solutions architecture. If you like evidence and careful investigation, compare evaluation with research or governance.

For any real opening, record the seniority, required background, location, customer contact, expected outputs, and evidence requested in interviews. The role title alone cannot answer those questions.

::: tip How to use these profiles
Each profile includes an **illustrative job brief** written for this project. It is not a live advertisement, a statement that an employer is hiring, or a promise about entry requirements. :::
