# AI product manager

**Decide which AI capabilities solve a meaningful user problem.** An AI product manager helps a team define the problem, choose priorities, establish success measures, and learn from deployment. Product management is an established field; AI changes some of the product's capabilities, failure modes, and economics.

The role is collaborative. Engineers, designers, researchers, analysts, domain experts, and users contribute expertise. A product manager helps connect that work and make decisions visible rather than owning every decision alone.

## What the work looks like

A product manager may discover that users need a faster way to review documents, then investigate whether AI can help without creating unacceptable errors. They define a useful workflow, identify which mistakes matter, and decide what evidence is needed before launch.

Typical responsibilities include interviews, problem statements, requirements, prioritization, coordinating experiments, setting quality thresholds, and planning feedback and support. AI-specific work can include evaluating whether model quality is sufficient, choosing where human review belongs, and understanding how cost and response time affect the experience.

Deliverables include a product brief, prioritized requirements, a measurement plan, an experiment or release decision, and a record of what the team learned. A compelling model demo is an input to that decision, not a substitute for understanding users.

## Skills to develop

- **Discovery:** interviews, workflow observation, problem framing, and distinguishing needs from suggested solutions.
- **Measurement:** product metrics, qualitative feedback, experiment design, and reading evaluation results critically.
- **Technical literacy:** model limitations, retrieval, latency, cost, data permissions, and integration constraints.
- **Execution:** writing clear specifications, prioritizing scope, and coordinating tradeoffs across disciplines.

Coding can help you prototype and communicate with engineers, but it is not the only route. Domain knowledge and evidence-based product decisions also matter.

## Illustrative job brief

::: info Example written for this atlas
Lead discovery for an AI feature that helps staff draft report summaries. Interview users, define where human approval is required, agree on quality criteria with engineers and domain experts, and plan a limited pilot. Track whether the feature saves useful time without increasing consequential errors.
:::

This sample is not a current job posting. Employers vary in how much technical depth and prior product experience they require.

## A realistic entry route

Practice product work in a student project, internship, or existing job: investigate a problem, make a focused proposal, and measure the outcome. Product operations, business analysis, UX research, software engineering, and domain-specific roles can provide relevant foundations. Associate product positions may offer a supervised route where available.

Avoid presenting a feature list as research. Record how you gathered feedback and what would cause you to cancel or change the idea.

## Portfolio proof

Create a case study with consented user interviews or explicitly simulated research. Include a problem statement, alternatives, a prototype, prioritized requirements, and a pilot plan. Define product success separately from model accuracy. Document accessibility needs, failure recovery, and who reviews generated material before it affects another person.

## How this role differs

An [AI engineer](/roles/ai-engineer) implements the feature, and an [evaluation engineer](/roles/evaluation-engineer) builds detailed evidence of system behavior. The product manager connects that evidence with user needs and product priorities. Strong teams make these choices together.
