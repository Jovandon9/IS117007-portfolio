# AI governance and safety specialist

**Help an organization understand AI risks and support accountable decisions.** This profile groups related work so you can explore it, but governance and technical safety are not interchangeable specialties. A governance analyst, AI security tester, model risk specialist, and safety researcher can have very different daily work.

Some roles focus on policies and organizational processes; others require substantial technical expertise. Read which risks, systems, and decisions the position actually owns.

## What the work looks like

A governance specialist may maintain an AI system inventory, organize risk assessments, document approval processes, and track whether controls have evidence behind them. They work with product, engineering, security, privacy, legal, and domain experts.

Technical safety and assurance work may involve testing for harmful behavior, misuse, information leakage, robustness, or unsafe tool use. The appropriate tests depend on the system and its intended context. A generic checklist is not enough to establish that a system is safe.

Typical deliverables include a risk register, a documented assessment, review criteria, testing evidence, monitoring requirements, and a clear record of responsibility. These processes should influence deployment decisions rather than become paperwork disconnected from actual behavior.

## Skills to develop

- **AI literacy:** model capabilities, data flows, limitations, evaluations, and how systems are deployed.
- **Risk practice:** identifying harms, assessing context, selecting controls, and keeping evidence traceable.
- **Communication:** precise documentation, cross-functional reviews, and explaining uncertainty without overstating assurance.
- **Specialist depth:** privacy, cybersecurity, policy, a regulated domain, statistics, or technical testing, depending on the role.

Legal and compliance requirements depend on jurisdiction and use case. Framework familiarity helps structure questions; it does not substitute for qualified legal advice or prove legal compliance.

## Illustrative job brief

::: info Example written for this atlas
Support the review of an internal AI summarization tool. Document its purpose, users, data sources, and owners; identify plausible risks; gather evaluation and security evidence; and propose controls and monitoring. Record unresolved issues and route the deployment decision to the responsible team.
:::

This is an illustrative scope, not a live opening. A technical safety research role could require very different experience from this governance-focused example.

## A realistic entry route

Relevant foundations include security, privacy, audit, policy analysis, data science, product operations, and domain expertise. Learn the technical basics of the systems you want to assess, then practice reviewing a concrete application. Technical safety positions may require specialized engineering or research experience.

Early-career opportunities may appear under risk, assurance, compliance, security, or responsible AI rather than an exact “AI governance” title. Compare actual responsibilities and supervision when researching openings.

## Portfolio proof

Assess a small public-data AI application. Create a system description, data-flow diagram, risk register, and a limited evaluation plan. Connect each proposed control to a specific risk and identify who would own it. Distinguish evidence you collected from assumptions and unanswered questions. Do not label the application “safe” or “compliant” on the basis of a student review.

## How this role differs

An [evaluation engineer](/roles/evaluation-engineer) may build the measurement tools that inform an assessment. A governance specialist connects evidence to organizational responsibilities and decisions. Technical safety researchers investigate specific failure mechanisms; those responsibilities need separate expertise even when teams collaborate closely.
