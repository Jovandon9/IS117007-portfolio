# Start your career research

The AI industry uses overlapping titles. An **AI engineer** at one employer might build a customer support assistant; another employer might use the same title for model training. Your first research task is to identify the work behind the title.

This site is an exploratory career guide. Its role profiles describe common patterns, and its sample job briefs are illustrative. They are **not live vacancies, hiring forecasts, or promises of a particular career outcome**. Use current employer job descriptions to test what you read here.

## 1. Pick a problem you would enjoy solving

Start with work rather than a fashionable title:

| I want to… | Explore first |
| --- | --- |
| Build useful software with existing models | [AI engineer](/roles/ai-engineer) |
| Work with customers to get AI systems into production | [Forward-deployed AI engineer](/roles/forward-deployed-engineer) |
| Improve data pipelines, training, and model serving | [Machine learning engineer](/roles/ml-engineer) |
| Measure failures and make model behavior more dependable | [AI evaluation engineer](/roles/evaluation-engineer) |

These are starting points. A small company may combine several of these responsibilities in one job.

## 2. Collect a small, comparable sample

Choose two role families and collect three to five recent employer job descriptions for each. Keep geography and seniority reasonably similar. Record the employer, exact title, location, publication date when available, and the date you accessed the page.

Prefer the employer's careers site over a repost. Save a short factual summary for your own research because postings can change or disappear. Do not describe an old posting as an open job without checking it again.

## 3. Read for responsibilities and evidence

For each description, highlight:

- **Outputs:** What would the person actually deliver—an API, an evaluation suite, a deployed workflow, or a product decision?
- **Technical work:** Does the role emphasize software integration, model training, data quality, infrastructure, or research?
- **Collaboration:** Who needs the work—customers, researchers, product managers, or internal teams?
- **Requirements:** Separate stated minimum qualifications from preferred experience. Record required years of experience without assuming they are flexible.
- **Constraints:** Note travel, on-call duty, location, work authorization, and security requirements.

Compare repeated responsibilities across postings. A title alone is weak evidence of what a whole occupation requires.

## 4. Turn findings into a learning decision

Choose one skill that appears repeatedly and build a small project that demonstrates it. For an AI application role, that might be a retrieval assistant with a measured failure rate. For an evaluation role, it might be a test set and a documented scoring method.

Use the [skills roadmap](/guide/skills-roadmap) to plan practice and the [portfolio projects](/guide/portfolio-projects) to define a manageable deliverable. Keep your first version small enough to finish and explain.

## 5. Revisit your conclusions

Job titles, tools, and employer expectations change. Recheck the evidence before applying, and distinguish your observations from general claims. A handful of postings can guide your next project; it cannot establish the size or growth rate of an entire job market.

The [sources and research worksheet](/guide/sources) will help you keep those distinctions visible.
