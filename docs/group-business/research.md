## Research question and method

How could a small student-facing service connect career exploration, an achievable learning task, and inspectable project evidence without promising employment or duplicating a full course platform?

Inspected three original learning-site pages on November 17, 2026 and Chase's repository at commit `3cfe1497a9c636eac009576458fd3ce9c289d9eb`. These are purposively selected substitutes/reference services, not a comprehensive competitive market survey. No accounts, purchases, interviews, customer trials or willingness-to-pay study were performed. Publication dates were not consistently supplied; dates here are check dates.

## Compare two business positions

| Position | Audience, offer and possible business model | Strength | Risk / evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Broad AI career discovery directory | Anyone exploring AI; curated role/job/learning summaries. A sponsorship model could be explored later. | Closest to the existing Atlas; inexpensive content-first prototype. | Broad maintenance burden; little differentiation from guides. Sponsorship interest and demand are unverified. |
| B — One project, one evidence review | Students who already code but need a bounded first AI portfolio artifact; scoped briefs and an inquiry for project feedback. A future fixed-fee review is only a hypothesis. | Connects a role requirement to something a learner can show; one complete inquiry task fits a small team. | Reviewer expertise, delivery capacity, student interest and willingness to pay are unknown. No fees, bookings or service guarantees should be presented as real. |

**Proposed direction: B.** Start with self-guided briefs and a clearly labeled demonstration inquiry. Do not sell reviews or claim qualified mentors exist. A service can be a realistic business prototype before its operating model is validated.

Change this direction if peer discovery finds learners mainly need basic programming support, cannot understand the brief, or prefer a free existing course; investigate before expanding.

## Two persona hypotheses

These are invented planning personas, not interviews, real students, or observed behavior. No simulated customer quotation is presented as genuine evidence.

| Persona hypothesis | Situation and need | Design implication / assumption to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary — Sam, undergraduate with Python/Git basics | Wants an AI-related project small enough to explain to an internship reviewer; has limited weekly time and uncertainty about proof of completion. | Show prerequisites, minimum deliverable, effort estimate and expected evidence before the inquiry action. Test whether Sam's supposed time/scope problem occurs with a real peer. |
| Secondary — Riley, experienced student programmer | Wants evaluation depth and reproducible results, not introductory setup repetition. | Offer optional extensions and raw-output/evaluation guidance while preserving the same small minimum. **Selected audience hypothesis:** undergraduate programmers choosing their first small applied-AI portfolio artifact. Narrowing by task and prerequisite is more useful than assuming all students share the same ability. Personal work authorization, grades, résumé details and other private circumstances are unnecessary for this research record.

## Brand, design and honest persuasion

Proposed promise: **Turn one career requirement into evidence you can explain.** Primary character: Sage (clear evidence and explanation), with a helpful mentor tone. Voice: specific, calm, candid. These are proposed brand decisions, not copied Brand Studio approvals.

Reuse the Brand Studio process—audience → promise → alternatives → check—rather than copying the T-shirt identity. No approved group logo or visual system was found.

Two visual approaches to compare during Plan:
1. A structured guide: restrained navy/teal, clear headings, evidence tables, role-to-brief path.
2. A project studio: warmer accent, example artifact cards, and a prominent first-project path.

Use identical core copy and task for comparison. These are design proposals; neither variant has been built or usability-tested.

Two honest persuasion choices:
- **Reciprocity:** provide a useful free project brief and rubric before requesting an inquiry. Avoid a hidden payment or compulsory signup to read the promised material.
- **Commitment/consistency:** let visitors choose one goal and see a small checklist they can revise or clear. Do not imply that choosing a goal creates a booking or makes them job-ready.

Use cited evidence and labeled limits for credibility. No fabricated testimonials, mentor credentials, job guarantees, artificial countdowns or unsupported scarcity.
